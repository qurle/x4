# Foam-board body (v1)

A rigid box for the rig made from **3 mm foam board**. The four cameras sit in one row with their
bodies touching, so the lenses are exactly one body width apart (24.1 mm). The Core sits in the
removable back door, the bank lies under a shelf, and air goes in at the sides and out at the top.

General enclosure notes (light, heat, v2 acrylic) are in [ENCLOSURE.md](ENCLOSURE.md). This page is
the build guide for v1.

Four A4 sheets at 1:1 scale, generated by `tools/enclosure_templates.py`:

| [sheet-2.svg](assets/body/sheet-2.svg) | B1–B3 camera frames, C retainer with vent windows |
| [sheet-3.svg](assets/body/sheet-3.svg) | F top (exhaust slots, shutter and switch marks), G bottom, I shelf, H1/H2 sides (intake slots) |
| [sheet-4.svg](assets/body/sheet-4.svg) | E1–E3 Core collar |

[templates.pdf](assets/body/templates.pdf) has all four sheets as one file.

How to print:
1. Print at **100 % / "actual size"**. Turn off "fit to page".
2. Measure the 50 mm bar and the 10 mm square on each sheet with a ruler. If either is off by more
   than 0.5 mm, fix the print settings before you cut anything.
3. Line types: **solid** = cut through, **dashed** = mark it from your real part and then cut,
   **+** = hole centre.

If your parts or your board measure differently from the table below, change the numbers at the
top of `tools/enclosure_templates.py` and run `python tools/enclosure_templates.py` again.

## Check before cutting

1. **Lens position.** Print sheet 1, hold a camera against the back of the paper with its Grove end
   down and its body pushed into the corner of the dashed area you expect, and look through the
   hole. The lens should sit in the middle of the circle. If it doesn't, change
   `CAM_LENS_FROM_GROVE` in the script.
2. **Picture orientation.** **Not sure** what "Grove end down" does to the picture. Before gluing,
   take one shot with a camera held that way and look at it. If it's upside down or sideways,
   either fix it in firmware or turn all four cameras the same way and regenerate the templates.
   All four must point the same way in any case.
3. **Measure the bank and the board.** If the bank is thicker than 25 mm, raise the shelf
   (`SHELF_Y`). If the board isn't 3.0 mm, set `T`.
4. **Find your switch and Mini Button.** The top panel only has dashed marks for them. Put the
   real parts on the printed top and draw around them.

## Tools and materials

- 3 mm foam board, A3 or bigger. Black board looks better and leaks less light (see
  [Light](#light)).
- Snap-off knife with **new blades**. Snap a segment every couple of panels: a blunt blade tears
  the foam.
- Steel ruler, ideally with a cork back; cutting mat.
- Compass cutter or circle cutter for the Ø14.5 lens holes (a sharp 14–15 mm hole punch also
  works).
- PVA (white wood glue) for laminating. A low-temperature hot glue gun for assembly.
- Paper masking tape, low-tack tape or repositionable spray glue to hold the templates.
- 240 and 400 grit sandpaper on a flat block.
- Matte black acrylic paint or a black marker, for hole edges.
- Hook-and-loop (Velcro) dots, cloth tape (book-binding or gaffer tape) for the door hinge.
- Optional: kraft paper or bookcloth for the outside, gesso or water-based primer.

**No solvent glue, spray paint or superglue.** Solvents melt the foam core. Superglue fumes fog
lenses (cyanoacrylate blooms white on plastic and glass).

## Cutting

- Hold the template on the board with low-tack tape or a light coat of repositionable spray, and
  cut through the paper.
- Cut in **three passes**: score the top paper, cut through the foam, then cut the bottom paper.
  Keep the blade vertical. Pressing hard in one pass crushes the foam and leaves a ragged edge.
- Inside corners: cut from the corner outwards, so an overcut ends up inside the scrap.
- Lens holes: cut them about 0.5 mm small first, then sand or ream them to a snug fit on a real
  lens boss.
- Cut the three camera frames B1–B3 **from one stack**: glue three rough blanks together first
  (see [Stiffness](#stiffness)), then cut the openings through all three. They will line up
  exactly.
- Test-fit every part before glue goes on it.

## Stiffness

The pictures only line up if the four cameras never move relative to each other. Most of the
stiffness comes from how the parts are glued, not from the board itself.

- **Laminated camera block.** Front panel A + frames B1–B3 glued flat with PVA = a 12 mm thick
  plate. That's many times stiffer than a single 3 mm sheet, and the cameras sit in it like eggs in
  a carton. Press it flat under books for a few hours. A warped block means misaligned cameras.
- **Shelf as a diaphragm.** The shelf I is glued to the front block and both sides. It stops the
  box racking (turning into a parallelogram) when you squeeze it.
- **Corner fillets.** Run a bead of hot glue along every inside corner, or glue in 5 × 5 mm
  strips of scrap foam. The joint then holds on the paper faces, not just the thin edge.
- **Doublers where screws or switches go.** Foam board doesn't hold threads or nuts. Glue a
  30 × 30 mm patch of 3 mm board inside the top under the switch and the button. For a tripod
  T-nut, glue a piece of 3 mm plywood or hardboard inside the bottom.
- **Paper grain.** The face paper bends more easily one way. If you can tell (flex a strip), cut
  the long parts (front, back, top, bottom) with the stiff direction along the 140 mm side.
- Optional: glue a thin steel ruler or a 3 mm plywood strip along the bottom bar of the camera
  block if it still flexes.

## Gluing

| joint | glue | why |
|---|---|---|
| laminating A + B1–B3, collar E1–E3, doublers | PVA, thin coat, press flat overnight | strong, slow enough to align, no heat |
| box corners: front block, sides, top, bottom, shelf | low-temp hot glue | quick; fillet the inside corner afterwards |
| cameras, flash, Core, bank | **no glue**: friction fit, retainer, hook-and-loop | you will need to take them out |
| outside skin (paper, bookcloth) | PVA brushed thin or a glue stick | wet glue on large areas warps the board |

- PVA soaks the paper and can make it bow. Use a thin coat, glue both sides of a joint the same
  way, and dry it under weight.
- Hot glue: use a **low-temperature** gun. A high-temperature gun melts the foam core and leaves a
  dent. Glue only on the paper faces and on cut edges, and hold each joint square for about 20
  seconds.
- Keep all glue at least 5 mm from a lens hole. Strings of hot glue in the light path ruin
  pictures.

## Light

The white foam core is translucent: flash light can travel through it into the lens holes.

- Paint every cut edge around the lens holes and the flash window **matte black**.
- The 4 mm divider between camera 4 and the flash is the only thing between them. Paint its edges
  too, or use black foam board.
- Inside the box, matte black paint or black paper on the faces near the flash keeps light out of
  the camera holes.
- Test: in a dark room, cover the lenses with your fingers outside the box, fire the flash and
  look for glow around the lens holes. Then take a picture of a dark wall with the flash on. A
  bright haze or ring means a leak.

## Vents and heat

The cameras' ESP32-S3 modules are rated for 65 °C ambient (**VERIFIED**, Espressif; see
[ENCLOSURE.md](ENCLOSURE.md)), and a closed foam box is a good insulator.

- Intake: 3 slots, 18 × 3 mm, in each side just above the shelf.
- Exhaust: 8 slots, 18 × 3 mm, in the top over the air channel. Warm air rises, so the air flows
  without a fan.
- Retainer C has a 14 × 14 window behind each camera so the backs of the modules see moving air.
- Cutting slots: cut the two long sides first, then the short ones, and push the piece out from
  behind. Paint the slot edges black (light) and seal them with a thin coat of PVA (fluff).
- Keep the slots clear: don't put tape or the switch wiring over them.
- If the soak test (below) gets too warm, add a second row of side slots or a 30 mm 5 V fan
  blowing out of the top. A fan draws power; measure it on the rail first.

## Assembly order

1. **Laminate the camera block.** Glue three rough 134 × 52 blanks into one stack, tape template
   B on top, and cut the openings and Grove notches through the stack. Then glue the front panel A
   on the front: the frame stack sits 3 mm in from A's left, right and top edges (that's where the
   sides and the top go). Put one camera in before the glue sets and check its lens is centred in
   its hole. Press flat and let it dry.
2. **Test-fit the cameras and the flash.** Each camera should slide in with light friction and its
   lens boss should sit in its hole. All four bodies touch: there is no foam between cameras.
3. **Laminate the collar** E1–E3 and glue it on the inside face of the door D around the dashed
   Core outline.
4. **Build the shell.** Glue the sides H1/H2 to the camera block, then the shelf I (front edge
   against the front panel, top face on the line marked on the sides), then the bottom G and the top F. Top and
   bottom run the full width, over the sides. Fillet every inside corner.
5. **Mount the parts.** Bank on hook-and-loop under the shelf. Breakout, capacitor and switch in
   the cable zone. Shutter button and switch through the top panel (doublers behind them). Cables
   go down through the 10 mm gap behind the shelf.
6. **Retainer.** Hold C against the camera backs with hook-and-loop dots, so it comes off when a
   camera needs its Grove cable or its G0 header.
7. **Door.** Put the Core in the collar, screen to the window, and hold it with a hook-and-loop
   strap across its back. Hinge the door along the top edge with a strip of cloth tape inside and
   out. Close it with a hook-and-loop tab or two small magnets at the bottom. Leave enough slack in
   the Core's Port A cable for the door to open.

What you can reach by opening the door: the Core's USB-C and SD slot, the cameras' Grove plugs
and G0 headers (after the retainer comes off), and the bank's plug.

## Finishing

- **Edges.** Sand cut edges lightly with 400 grit on a block, then cover them with paper tape, or
  fill them with a thin coat of PVA or acrylic filler and sand again when dry.
- **Corners.** Wrap each outside corner with a strip of paper tape before any skin goes on. It
  hides the foam and makes the corner tougher.
- **Skin.** Kraft paper, bookcloth or coloured card glued on with a thin coat of PVA or a glue
  stick gives the box a finished look and protects it from scuffs. Do one face at a time and
  smooth it from the middle out.
- **Paint.** Prime with gesso or a water-based primer first, then use water-based acrylic.
  **Never spray paint**: the propellant dissolves the foam.
- **Lens holes.** Matte black edges (see [Light](#light)). A short black card tube around each
  lens works as a hood if stray light still shows.
- **Labels.** Number the lens holes 1–4 on the inside, matching the camera IDs.

## Tests

1. **Flex.** Hold the box by its ends and twist gently. The front block shouldn't visibly bend.
   If it does, add the plywood or ruler stiffener.
2. **Alignment.** Shoot a grid or a brick wall about 2 m away and compare the four pictures. The
   picture-to-picture offset should be the same every time you reassemble the box.
3. **Light leak.** The dark-room test in [Light](#light).
4. **Heat.** Run the soak ([BRINGUP step 8](BRINGUP.md#step-8--soak)) with the door closed. Touch
   the top over the exhaust slots: warm is fine, hot is not.
5. **Drop and wiggle.** Repeat the power checks from [SOLDERING.md](SOLDERING.md) with everything
   in the box. Cables get bent differently once the door is closed.
